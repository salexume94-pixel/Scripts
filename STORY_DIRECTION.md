# Story Direction

## Purpose

This document records the high-level narrative direction for the Scripts RPG. It is intentionally separate from implementation details and from the eventual story content.

The goal is to establish the game's narrative identity before building specific quests, NPC dialogue, chapters, or major plot events.

## Core Premise

The game takes place in a fantasy MMORPG-style world.

The protagonist is an ordinary person who is beginning the game and logging into the MMO for the first time. The opening introduces the player to the game's world through a small tutorial settlement and ordinary beginner gameplay.

The protagonist is **not initially presented as a chosen one, legendary hero, or uniquely destined person**.

The player begins by simply playing the game.

## Opening Direction

The game should initially feel like a normal fantasy RPG/MMO.

The opening should introduce the player naturally through gameplay:

- Character enters the MMO world for the first time.
- Player begins in a small tutorial town.
- Town contains basic services and NPCs.
- Player receives a simple beginner quest.
- Player learns movement, exploration, combat, equipment, items, and other core systems naturally.
- The player leaves town and encounters the first area outside the settlement.

The initial story should not immediately reveal the central mystery.

## Tutorial Town

The first settlement should function as both a tutorial area and the player's first meaningful connection to the world.

Possible core locations include:

- Inn
- Item/equipment shop
- Church, shrine, statue, or Waystone
- Town exit
- A small number of NPCs
- Quest-giving NPCs
- Nearby beginner area

The healing/save location can eventually become more important to the story and world lore.

## Narrative Hook

The first quests should appear ordinary.

The player should gradually encounter small inconsistencies that do not fit the apparent rules of the MMO.

Examples of possible early anomalies:

- An enemy appears where it should not.
- An item exists that should not be obtainable.
- An NPC behaves in a way that conflicts with the game's expected rules.
- The player discovers an area that should not exist.
- A quest behaves differently from what the game normally allows.
- An unexplained event occurs during an otherwise ordinary quest.

These anomalies should initially be subtle.

The player should notice that something is wrong before they understand what is wrong.

## Intended Story Progression

The overall progression should move gradually through several perceptions:

1. **"I'm playing an MMO."**
2. **"Something about this game is strange."**
3. **"This world isn't behaving like a normal MMO."**
4. **"There is something underneath this world."**
5. **"What exactly is this world?"**
6. **"What should I do now that I know the truth?"**

The mystery should develop through normal gameplay rather than relying primarily on long exposition or constant cutscenes.

## Story Influences

The desired tone and storytelling approach draws inspiration from the types of games the developer enjoys, including:

- Pokémon
- Shin Megami Tensei
- Persona
- Baldur's Gate
- Dark Souls
- Elden Ring
- NieR: Automata
- Final Fantasy
- Chrono Trigger
- Chrono Cross
- Breath of Fire
- .hack
- Sword Art Online

These are influences for structure, atmosphere, mystery, character development, worldbuilding, and thematic depth. The game should not directly reproduce their stories.

## Storytelling Philosophy

The player should discover the story while playing the game.

Gameplay should carry narrative information whenever possible.

Quests, exploration, NPC interactions, combat encounters, locations, items, and changes to the world should all be capable of revealing pieces of the larger story.

The story should begin small and personal before gradually expanding into larger questions.

## Protagonist Direction

The protagonist begins as an ordinary player.

Their importance should develop through their actions and discoveries rather than being established through an immediate prophecy or predetermined destiny.

The story can eventually give the protagonist a meaningful role in the larger conflict, but that role should be earned through the player's journey.

## Quest Relationship

Quests should provide the player's immediate goals.

The larger story should provide the long-term direction and mystery.

A useful distinction is:

- **Quest:** "What am I doing right now?"
- **Story:** "Why does this matter?"
- **Mystery:** "What is actually happening?"
- **Choice:** "What do I do about it?"

Quest progression and story progression should remain separate systems while being able to communicate with each other.

For example:

Quest completed -> Story milestone -> New dialogue/world state -> New quest

## Current Story Development Stage

The story is intentionally **not fully written yet**.

The next narrative-design task is to develop the tutorial town, its important NPCs, and the first quest while preserving the central mystery.

The first quest should establish the game's tone and teach core gameplay systems without revealing the larger truth.

## Future Development

The following should be designed after the initial direction is established:

1. Tutorial town
2. Initial NPCs
3. First quest
4. First wilderness area
5. First anomaly
6. Early story milestones
7. Major characters
8. Regions and their individual story arcs
9. Central mystery
10. Main conflict
11. Major choices and consequences
12. Final confrontation
13. Ending conditions

This document should be expanded as the story is developed rather than replaced with unrelated implementation details.
